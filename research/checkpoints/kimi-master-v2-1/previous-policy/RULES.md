# Evidence and writing policy

## Claims

Prefer versioned official rules and organizer documents, then institutional publications, team records and repositories, and attributable reporting. Kimi output is imported research, not independent verification.

Distinguish source facts, field observations, analysis, hypotheses, and recommendations. Attribute single-source and team-reported claims. Preserve uncertainty, contradictions, and negative evidence. Never infer causality, participation, affiliation, or adoption from proximity or chronology alone.

Record each consequential claim against Evidence records, with the source locator, date, scope, confidence, and verification status. A search seed or transcription is a lead until checked against the underlying material. Non-public material cannot be the only support for a public factual claim.

## Sources and citations

Record publisher/creator, title, publication date (or explicitly unknown), direct URL or imported-file locator, access date for web material, and version/commit where relevant. A Kimi ID is not a substitute for these details.

Use claim-level clickable citations `[[n]](#ref-n)` with a matching `<a id="ref-n"></a>` in the same page. References link to specific underlying documents. Reader-facing prose must not contain bare URLs. Preserve source titles and quotations faithfully.

## Comparisons and rights

Important numbers require date, population, definition, unit, and source. Keep organizations, teams, entries, people, and robots as separate denominators. Do not combine incompatible measurements.

Public visibility, source availability, open-source licensing, reproducibility, reuse, and adoption are separate claims. Record explicit license evidence and version; unknown rights remain unknown. Screen personal, confidential, and restricted material before committing imported files or publishing derived content.

## Writing

Write concrete, reader-first prose in the language agreed for drafting. Explain documented examples before generalizing. Give each topic one home in the evolving report plan and cross-reference it elsewhere. Do not discard useful evidence merely to shorten prose. Source archives are not ready-to-publish chapters.
